01 / Overview

Understanding the Customer Behind the Numbers

A beauty and personal care business in Kenya was experiencing challenges with customer acquisition, limited consumer understanding and insufficient visibility into the effectiveness of its marketing activities.

At the beginning of the engagement, the business reported an average of approximately four customers per week, against an owner-defined target of 15.

While marketing activities were taking place, the business lacked a structured approach to understanding its customers, informing channel decisions and connecting marketing activity to commercial outcomes.

MIPOL was engaged through a paid pilot to investigate these challenges and develop a more evidence-informed approach to marketing.

The engagement progressed through two stages:

  1. Diagnostic and consumer intelligence: Understanding the business, its customers, audience preferences and existing marketing challenges.
  2. Implementation and impact measurement: Translating research findings into marketing decisions, implementing recommendations and establishing a framework for evaluating marketing performance and commercial response.

During the engagement, reported weekly customer volume increased from approximately four to approximately 12. This represented a 200% increase against the reported starting baseline, reaching 80% of the owner's weekly customer target.

These are directional observations during the engagement and should not be interpreted as independently established causal effects of individual marketing activities.
02 / Business context

The Business Behind the Challenge

The business operated in the competitive beauty and personal care market, offering services including makeup, nails, lashes and hair treatments.

Its intended customer segments included Gen Z and millennial women. Although these segments represented potential customers, the business had limited insight into their preferences, discovery behaviour, media consumption and motivations when choosing beauty services.

Marketing decisions therefore needed a stronger foundation in consumer understanding.

03 / The challenge

Marketing Activity Without Sufficient Consumer Intelligence

The initial diagnostic identified several challenges affecting the business's marketing effectiveness.

Limited consumer understanding

The business lacked sufficient insight into its intended customers, including the platforms they used, their preferences and what influenced their decisions.

Unclear channel prioritisation

There was limited consumer evidence to inform where marketing attention and investment should be directed.

Creative and messaging gaps

The business needed communication that reflected the interests, expectations and motivations of its different audience segments.

Fragmented performance measurement

There was no consistent framework connecting marketing expenditure and audience engagement to enquiries, bookings, completed appointments and revenue.

Limited commercial visibility

The business needed a better understanding of customer acquisition, repeat visits and which services were contributing to demand.

The challenge was therefore not simply to increase marketing activity, but to improve the intelligence informing marketing decisions.

04 / Stage One: Diagnostic & Consumer Intelligence

Understanding the Audience Before Making Marketing Decisions

MIPOL began with a diagnostic to understand the business and its target consumers before recommending changes to marketing activity.

The diagnostic examined:

  • Existing marketing activities and customer acquisition.
  • Audience segmentation and consumer preferences.
  • Digital platform usage across Gen Z and millennials.
  • Service positioning and differentiation.
  • Creative relevance and communication.
  • Existing performance tracking and commercial information gaps.

Consumer survey findings

A consumer survey was conducted to understand the platforms used by the two intended customer segments.

Both Gen Z and millennials identified TikTok as a platform they used.
This provided an evidence-based input into channel prioritisation. Rather than assuming that different generations necessarily required entirely different digital platforms, the findings suggested an opportunity to prioritise a shared channel while adapting creative concepts and messaging to their respective motivations.

Diagnostic output: Consumer insights, identified marketing gaps and prioritised recommendations to inform subsequent implementation.

05 / From intelligence to recommendations

Translating Consumer Understanding into Marketing Decisions

Based on the diagnostic findings, MIPOL developed recommendations across five areas.

  1. Audience-informed channel prioritisation: Prioritise TikTok as a key digital channel based on the survey findings, with greater attention allocated to the platform within the marketing approach.
  2. Audience-relevant creative and messaging: Move away from generic communication towards content that reflects audience interests and motivations. Gen Z: relatable, entertaining and culturally relevant content. Millennials: quality, confidence, convenience and service credibility.
  3. Complementary offline visibility: Use local flyer distribution to support awareness and reach potential customers within the surrounding region.
  4. Clearer service differentiation: Improve communication around available services, their benefits and reasons customers should choose the business.
  5. Improved marketing measurement: Introduce a more structured approach to monitoring customer acquisition, audience response, marketing expenditure and commercial outcomes.

The recommendations provided a basis for moving from assumptions towards more informed marketing execution.

06 / Stage Two: Implementation & Marketing Investment

Putting Consumer Intelligence into Practice

Following the diagnostic, MIPOL supported the implementation of the recommendations. The consumer survey informed the decision to prioritise TikTok as the main digital channel.

Most TikTok activity was organic rather than dependent on paid advertising. The emphasis was on creative relevance, audience-aligned messaging and content designed to encourage engagement and discovery.

TikTok: Organic Audience Engagement

Implementation focused on:

  • Developing creative concepts informed by audience interests.
  • Adapting messaging to different audience motivations.
  • Producing relatable and engaging content.
  • Communicating service offerings more clearly.
  • Monitoring content performance and audience response.

The intention was to make the content relevant to the people the business wanted to attract, rather than relying primarily on paid distribution to generate visibility.

Flyers: Local Market Awareness

Offline marketing complemented digital activity through flyer design and production, distribution within the surrounding region, increased local visibility and additional opportunities for potential customers to discover the business.

These channels provided complementary routes to audience discovery: organic digital engagement and local offline awareness.

07 / Marketing impact & performance measurement

Connecting Marketing Activity to Business Outcomes

As implementation progressed, MIPOL introduced a structured measurement approach to understand how marketing activities were performing and what could be improved.

The objective was to move beyond reporting content engagement and establish a clearer connection between marketing activity, customer response and commercial outcomes.

A. Organic creative effectiveness

TikTok performance was assessed using available indicators:

  • Video views.
  • Likes, shares and reposts.
  • Saves and comments, where available.
  • Profile visits.
  • Enquiries and booking interest.

These indicators helped assess whether the creative and messaging were attracting attention and generating audience interaction.

Comments and enquiries were treated, where available, as early intent signals: comments can reveal questions and interest, while enquiries indicate active consideration. Tracking these alongside customer feedback, repeat visits and local awareness measures can help assess whether engagement is contributing to the salon's longer-term brand health; comments and enquiries alone do not prove stronger brand health.

B. Offline marketing response

  • Number of flyers produced and distributed.
  • Distribution locations.
  • Customer enquiries referencing flyers.
  • Customer-reported discovery through offline activity.

C. Commercial performance

  • New and returning customers.
  • Customer enquiries and bookings.
  • Completed appointments.
  • Services purchased and revenue generated.

D. Marketing investment efficiency

The framework also introduced measures for evaluating costs associated with content production, flyer design, printing and distribution.

The framework distinguishes between engagement, reported customer discovery and verified commercial conversion.

09 / Strategic interpretation

From Marketing Activity to Better Investment Decisions

The engagement highlighted an important distinction between conducting marketing activities and understanding their effectiveness.

1. Consumer intelligence should inform marketing investment

Research provided a stronger basis for channel prioritisation. Understanding where intended customers spend time helps businesses make more informed marketing decisions.

2. Creative relevance matters

Platform selection alone does not guarantee performance. The content, messaging and relevance to the intended audience also influence whether marketing attracts attention and encourages engagement.

3. Marketing measurement should inform the next decision

Views and engagement are useful indicators, but they are not substitutes for customer acquisition and commercial performance. Businesses need to understand what happens after attention is generated, from initial discovery to enquiry, booking and completed purchase.

The next opportunity is to strengthen channel-level attribution and determine the relative commercial contribution of digital and offline activities.
